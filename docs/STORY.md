# FATHOM: the story

Everything the game tells you about its world, in one place: the premise,
the people, what happened before the game starts, what the player meets on
the way down, how it ends, and the exact text of every log and story line.
The last section says which file each piece of text lives in, so any of it
can be rewritten.

## The premise in one breath

A research station at the bottom of the deepest trench on Earth went silent.
Its sonar beacon started pinging again, and something down there has been
pinging back. You are a salvage pilot sent down to strip the station. Your
dives take you through five depths of ocean to the trench floor. On the way
you recover the station crew's logs and piece together what they found:
something vast and very old below the trench floor that shapes the rock by
singing to it. It is not a monster. It has been alone in the dark for longer
than there have been words, and the crew were the first thing that ever sang
back. At the bottom you do not kill it. You answer it until it stops singing
at you and starts singing with you.

The tagline on the title screen is **"Light reveals. Sound betrays."** That is
both the core mechanic and the theme: the dark is safe, light and noise
draw attention, and in the end the thing that saves you is making the right
sound at the right time.

## Tone

Quiet, lonely and eerie rather than gory. It is closer to deep-sea wonder
than to horror, though the dark is dangerous. The creatures are animals, not
villains. The biggest presence in the game turns out to be lonely, not evil.
The logs are short and dry, written by professionals, and get more personal
as things go wrong.

## The setting

**Meridian Deep** is a four-person research station on the floor of the
deepest trench on Earth, at 10,912 metres. The trench is never named. The
depth is close to the real Challenger Deep, but nothing else ties it to a
real place. The crew had ninety days of supplies.

The dive passes through five biomes, two zones each, then the floor:

| Zones | Biome | Tagline (shown when you enter) |
|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | Sunlit Shelf | "Where the light still reaches." |
| 3-4 | Twilight Reef | "The last blue, and the first lights." |
| 5-6 | Midnight Caverns | "Nothing down here has ever seen the sun." |
| 7-8 | Abyssal Vents | "The sea floor is breathing fire." |
| 9-10 | Hadal Trench | "Something down here is singing." |
| 11 | The hadal floor | "the signal is close" |

A guardian seals the way down at the end of three biomes: the **Lamplighter**
at the bottom of the Twilight Reef, the **Blind Mother** at the bottom of the
Midnight Caverns, and the **Ironclad** at the bottom of the Abyssal Vents.

Between zones the sub stops at a **decompression stop**, a relay with a
salvage locker. There the pilot fits a module, repairs and recharges for
salvage, and hears a forecast of the next zone. Surfacing banks the salvage.
Losing the sub costs it.

## The player

An unnamed salvage pilot, never seen and never speaking, working a salvage
contract on Meridian Deep: "The salvage contract is yours." The game never
says who hired them. Each dive is one attempt; a lost sub's wreck waits
where it sank, with its cargo, to be recovered on a later dive. The pilot
is not there to rescue anyone. Okafor's last message speaks to them
directly: "Do not come to rescue me. Come to listen."

Their first sub, the Lantern, is a deep research submersible: its hull is
marked LANTERN, with the designation DSV-7 and the placard "TEST DEPTH
11 000 M" (the hadal floor lies at about 10 900 m). Whose seventh DSV it was
is as open as who hired the pilot.

The Mako found derelict in the reef is marked MAKO, PROTOTYPE, with the
designation XP-2 and a RESCUE arrow by its canopy: an experimental racer
someone built and lost, its second prototype at least.

The old Bathysphere in the caverns is stencilled B-3, TESTED 1 100 ATM, and
DO NOT OPEN UNDER PRESSURE by its hatch: a cast sphere far older than
Meridian, its paint flaking to rust, an observer's leather cap and goggles
on the seat inside.

The Whisper in the vents is a stealth survey drone with room for one pilot:
a flat, chined hull tiled in anechoic coating, marked only in low-visibility
grey (WHISPER, the designation SD-4, and a placard reading ANECHOIC COATING -
DO NOT PAINT), a smoked visor where a canopy would be, and violet light
along its chines. A few of its tiles are missing.

## The crew

- **Commander R. Hale.** Station commander, practical and protective. Hale
  writes the first log and the hardest ones: the order to go silent, and
  the night the trench closed round the escape lock. Hale leaves in the last
  escape pod with Sato.
- **Dr. L. Sato.** Acoustician. Sato finds the tone, runs the ping test,
  works out that the thing below shapes rock with sound, and names it "the
  Choir". Sato also logs the Blind Mother on the hydrophones. Sato leaves
  with Hale.
- **T. Vasquez.** Engineer and drone pilot, flying drone MD-02. Vasquez is
  first to notice the trench moving. He flies the drone at the Lamplighter's
  light and loses it. On the last night he goes out in the drone to cut a
  way past the sealed escape lock. The rock closes over the tunnel he is in.
  His drone is still down there (the MD-02 set piece), and its flight
  recorder holds his last words.
- **Dr. I. Okafor.** Biologist. She catalogues the fauna, notices them
  gathering like an audience, names the Lamplighter and the Mother, and
  breaks the silence order to play the thing a lullaby her mother sang. It
  sings it back, and then sings the crew's own voices. She decides it is
  lonely, not hostile, and stays behind when the others leave. She is the
  one who keeps the beacon going and leaves the message for whoever comes.

## What happened at Meridian Deep (before the game)

In story order, which is the order the logs are found:

1. **Day 1.** The station is running. It is the quietest place on Earth;
   Okafor can hear her own pulse in the hull.
2. Sato picks up a **steady 14 Hz tone from below the trench floor**, which
   should be impossible. It is logged as a thermal artefact. Sato does not
   believe that.
3. The **ping test**: the crew ping the source, and eleven seconds later
   their own ping comes back slowed down and stretched into something like a
   word. Hale asks Sato to keep it quiet.
4. Vasquez finds the **trench moving**. A surveyed tunnel has sealed, and a
   new chamber has opened in solid rock, with smooth walls "like they grew".
5. Okafor sees the fauna gather round the station at night like an
   **audience**: hunters and jellies hanging still, facing the floor,
   turning together when the tone changes pitch.
6. A light at the bottom of the reef: **the Lamplighter**. Vasquez flies the
   drone at it with lamps on and loses it. The crew learn she sees only light
   and fly dark past the reef after that.
7. Sato's **resonance hypothesis**: rock has resonant frequencies, so sing the
   right chord at a cave and perhaps it flows. The thing below is not living
   in the trench, it is composing it. Sato names it **the Choir**.
8. Hale's **standing order**: no sonar, minimum engines, no lights outside.
   The walls stop moving. Hale wants the thing to forget they are there.
9. Okafor breaks the order and plays a **lullaby** through the hull speakers.
   It sings the lullaby back perfectly, then sings the four crew members'
   voices, talking about it.
10. Sato logs **the Blind Mother**, an eyeless giant in the caverns under the
    reef that strikes at sound so hard it sticks in the rock. The Choir's song
    doesn't move her; she listens to the crew instead.
11. **The trench closes round the escape lock** at 0300. Vasquez goes out in
    the drone to cut a path. Six minutes of comms, then the Choir sings and
    his tunnel is simply not there any more.
12. Hale and Sato take **the last escape pod** up. Okafor stays: "I don't think
    it is hostile. I think it is lonely."
13. Okafor's **beacon message**, for whoever comes next: don't come to rescue
    me, come to listen; it tests everything that sings; answer it, keep
    answering.

Then, from the prologue: **forty days before the game starts the station went
silent; three weeks later its beacon started pinging again, and something
down there has been pinging back.** That is the hook that brings the salvage
contract.

## What the Choir is

The game deliberately never shows it whole. What the player can piece
together:

- It lives **below the trench floor**, where there should be no "below".
- It **sings**, and the song shapes the rock (the resonance hypothesis): it
  grows tunnels shut and opens chambers. In the Hadal zones you watch it do
  this around you. The Vasquez recorder says the moving rock feels "soft and
  warm, like it hasn't set yet", and the tunnel closes "slow, gentle, like a
  hand. It isn't angry. It's tidying up."
- It **mimics**: it sang back Okafor's lullaby and the crew's voices, and it
  answers pings with the pinger's own ping, slowed and stretched.
- It **holds the fauna** with its tone: the audience over the station.
- It **tests** whatever sings to it. The fight is that test: the waves are
  questions, pings are answers. Missing a wave hurts, answering costs nothing.
- It is **not hostile and not dying**. It is lonely. The ending is it being
  answered for longer than it ever has been.

What it physically is: a colossal glowing heart deep in the dark with long
tentacles trailing into the rock, and four voice organs at the edges of its
arena. Nothing explains where it came from. The guardians (the Lamplighter,
the Mother, the Ironclad) are not its servants, just the biggest animals in
their biomes. The Mother is explicitly unmoved by the song. The hunters that
appear mid-fight are called by the song.

## The way down: what the player meets

Story beats and set pieces, in dive order:

- **First dive only: the prologue**, a few lines over black while the first
  zone loads (full text below).
- **Signal logs**: one per zone, 80% of the time, always the next unfound log
  in story order. The hadal floor always has one. There are 13, and finding
  all of them changes the ending text (see below).
- **Sunlit Shelf**: shelf mantas with fish sheltering under their wings; in
  the second zone a sperm whale passes behind you, its clicks charting the
  cave on your sonar.
- **Twilight Reef**: giant clams with glowing pearls, sometimes a whale fall.
  The Mako prototype's derelict lies in the first reef zone. At the bottom is
  **the Lamplighter**: "She sees only light. Run dark, and dazzle her up close."
- **Midnight Caverns**: the glow-worm grotto, whose lights go dark at sound.
  The old Bathysphere's derelict lies in the first cavern zone. At the bottom
  is **the Blind Mother**: "She hunts by sound alone. Make her strike the rock."
- **Abyssal Vents**: a seam of singing glass that three rising pings shatter.
  This is the resonance hypothesis made playable, and the Choir asks the
  same of you at the bottom. The Whisper drone's derelict lies in the first
  vent zone. At the bottom is **the Ironclad**: "Its plates turn everything.
  The vents do not."
- **Hadal Trench**: the rock moves when the Choir sings, three times a zone.
  A tunnel behind you flows shut, a chamber opens ahead, or the way itself
  bends round. The **echo** answers your pings eleven seconds later, as it
  did for Sato. In the last trench zone is **MD-02**, Vasquez's drone, grown
  over by rock. Its flight recorder plays his last words.
- **The hadal floor**: the ruins of **Meridian Deep**, beacon still pinging,
  with an **audience** of hunters hanging over it, facing the floor and
  turning together when the tone shifts. Stay a while and the station's
  speakers play **Okafor's lullaby**, and something far below sings it
  back. Then the arena and **the Choir**.

Other recurring things with light story attached: **Meridian's survey
probes** (lost instruments that pay research), **your own lost wreck** from
a previous dive, and the **derelict subs** (see the gaps below).

## The finale

The Choir's song comes in three parts:

1. **The Call.** Banner: "THE CHOIR - Answer it. Keep answering." Song waves
   roll out from the heart. Answer each with a ping as it arrives. Every
   answer opens its four voice organs for a moment, and you harpoon them.
2. **The Reach.** Banner: "THE REACH - Its voices are everywhere now. Answer,
   and strike the heart." With its organs gone, the heart surges forward out
   of the dark. Waves come from voices around the arena, tentacles rise and
   slam down, and each answer opens the heart's throat.
3. **The Duet.** Banner: "THE DUET - It has stopped singing at you. It is
   singing with you. Keep answering." Wounded, the heart falls silent and
   listens. Its hunters hang still. It sings on, faster and faster, and twelve
   answers in a row end the song. Its light shifts toward the colour of your
   answers as the duet builds.

Then the final transmission plays (full text below), and the ending: the
climb back to the light, the epilogue lines, and, for pilots who found every
Meridian log, three extra lines about what became of the crew.

The intended reading: **you didn't kill it.** The song did not end, it
changed. The trench holds still behind you. Okafor stayed to listen, and now
it is listening too.

## Full text

### Prologue (first dive only)

> Forty days ago Meridian Deep, a research station on the floor of the deepest trench on Earth, went silent.
>
> Three weeks later its sonar beacon began to ping again.
>
> Something down there has been pinging back.
>
> The salvage contract is yours.

### The Meridian logs, in the order they are found

1. **Meridian Deep - Day 1** (Cmdr. R. Hale)
   "Meridian Deep is pressurised and operational at 10,912 metres. Four crew, ninety days of supplies, and the quietest place on Earth. Okafor says the silence down here is so complete she can hear her own pulse in the hull."
2. **Acoustic survey 03** (Dr. L. Sato)
   "There is a tone under the station. Fourteen hertz, steady, coming from below the floor of the trench - which should be impossible, because there is no below. I have logged it as a thermal artefact. I do not believe it is a thermal artefact."
3. **Ping test** (Dr. L. Sato)
   "We pinged the source. Eleven seconds later something pinged back. It was our own signal, slowed down and stretched into something that sounded like a word. Hale has asked me not to tell the others yet."
4. **Survey discrepancy** (T. Vasquez)
   "Ran the drone along yesterday's survey route and the route isn't there any more. Tunnel B has sealed. There's a new chamber where solid rock was. Nothing collapsed - the walls are smooth, like they grew. The trench is moving."
5. **Field notes** (Dr. I. Okafor)
   "The fauna gathers around the station at night. Lancers, jellies, things I have no names for, all facing the floor, perfectly still. Like an audience. Every time the tone changes pitch, they turn together."
6. **The light on the reef** (Dr. I. Okafor)
   "There is a light at the bottom of the reef, brighter than anything I have catalogued. Vasquez flew the drone at it with its lamps on full. The light went out a second before the drone did. Whatever holds it sees by light alone. We fly dark past the reef now, and I have started calling her the Lamplighter."
7. **Resonance hypothesis** (Dr. L. Sato)
   "Rock has resonant frequencies. Sing the right note at a wine glass and it shatters; sing the right chord at a cave and perhaps it flows. Whatever is down there is not living in the trench. It is composing it. I have started calling it the Choir."
8. **Standing order** (Cmdr. R. Hale)
   "Effective immediately: no active sonar. Engines at minimum. Lights off outside the hull. Since we went quiet the walls have stopped moving. The thing below can hear us, and I would like it to forget we are here."
9. **Unauthorised transmission** (Dr. I. Okafor)
   "I broke Hale's order tonight. I played it a melody through the hull speakers - the first thing I could think of, a lullaby my mother sang. It sang it back. Perfectly. And then it sang our voices. All four of us. Talking about it."
10. **Hydrophone log** (Dr. L. Sato)
    "Something enormous lives in the caverns under the reef. It has no eyes. I watched it on sonar strike the exact spot where Vasquez dropped a wrench, so hard it was stuck in the rock for a minute. The Choir's song does not move it. It listens to us instead. Okafor wants to call it the Mother."
11. **Emergency log** (Cmdr. R. Hale)
    "The trench closed around the escape lock at 0300. Vasquez went out in the drone to cut us a path. We heard him on comms for six minutes. Then the Choir sang, and the tunnel he was in was simply not there any more."
12. **Last entry, Okafor** (Dr. I. Okafor)
    "Hale and Sato took the last pod up. I stayed. I don't think it is hostile. I think it is lonely. It has been singing alone in the dark for longer than we have had words, and we are the first thing that ever sang back."
13. **Beacon message** (Dr. I. Okafor)
    "If you are hearing this, the beacon is still pinging, and someone came down. Do not come to rescue me. Come to listen. It will test you - it tests everything that sings. Answer it. Keep answering."

### Outside the Meridian set

- **Drone recorder** (T. Vasquez), played at MD-02 in the last trench zone:
  "Cutter's through the first seal. The rock is soft here, and warm, like it hasn't set yet. I can hear it through the frame - not the tone, a tune. The tunnel is closing behind me. Slow. Gentle, like a hand. It isn't angry, Hale. It's tidying up. Tell Okafor she was ri-"
- **Unknown transmission** (the Choir / I. Okafor), played when the song ends:
  "You answered. You kept answering. It has never been answered for that long. It is not dying - it is listening now, and the trench is holding still. Go up. Tell them the sea is not empty. Tell them it sings."

The final transmission is signed by both the Choir and Okafor on purpose. It
is in her voice, but whether she is speaking, or it is speaking in her voice
the way it mimicked the crew, is left open.

### Ending

The epilogue, over the climb back to the light:

> The song did not end. It changed.
>
> For the first time in longer than there have been words, something at the bottom of the world is listening.
>
> The trench holds still behind you as you rise.
>
> Somewhere below, a beacon keeps pinging - slower now, almost patient. Waiting for the next voice to answer.

Only for pilots who recovered every Meridian log:

> Hale and Sato's pod was never found. Neither was Vasquez's drone.
>
> But woven through the last song were four human voices, and one of them was humming a lullaby.
>
> Okafor stayed to listen. She is listening still. And now, so is it.

And if the lullaby was heard at the station: "As you rise, something far below
is still humming Okafor's lullaby."

### Set-piece lines

- Meridian Deep: banner "MERIDIAN DEEP - Its beacon is still pinging." Then "The station's speakers crackle into life: Okafor's lullaby, still playing." and "Something far below sings it back. Perfectly."
- The audience: "Hunters hang over the station, facing the floor, perfectly still. Do not wake them."
- MD-02: banner "MD-02 - Vasquez's drone. The rock has grown over its cockpit."
- The echo: "Eleven seconds later, something far below pinged back."
- Guardian banners: "She sees only light. Run dark, and dazzle her up close." / "She hunts by sound alone. Make her strike the rock." / "Its plates turn everything. The vents do not." When one falls: "THE WAY DOWN IS OPEN", with "The Lamplighter's light goes out." / "The Blind Mother is silent." / "The Ironclad's shell has gone cold."

### Wonders (the hangar's "witnessed" list)

A shelf manta, a sperm whale, a giant clam's pearl, a whale fall, the
glow-worm grotto, singing glass, the echo, the moving trench, MD-02, the
audience, and Okafor's lullaby. Each has a line of description, and the
later ones point back to the log they echo.

## Gaps and open questions (good places to tweak)

These are things the story currently leaves loose, or that newer gameplay
added without story:

- **The Ironclad has no log.** The Lamplighter and the Mother each have one;
  the third guardian has nothing. A Sato or Vasquez log about "something
  armoured walking the vents" would round it out.
- **The three derelict subs have no story.** The Mako prototype (reef), the
  old Bathysphere (caverns) and the Whisper drone (vents) were added as the
  way to unlock subs. Who lost them, and when? Earlier expeditions? Meridian's
  own supply runs? Each could carry a short recording.
- **Survey probes have no text.** They are Meridian's, and could each hold a
  one-line data fragment (temperatures rising, the tone shifting).
- **Who restarted the beacon?** Okafor implies she kept it running, but it
  restarted three weeks after the station went silent. Was it her, or the
  Choir copying the beacon's ping the way it copies everything?
- **Is Okafor alive?** Deliberately unanswered. The final transmission and the
  "four human voices" line lean toward "the Choir carries her voice now".
- **Hale and Sato's pod** "was never found". Did the Choir let them go?
- **Who hired the pilot?** "The salvage contract is yours" is all the game
  says. The contracts in the hangar (handicaps for bigger payouts) could
  name a client.
- **The trench is unnamed.** It could be named, or left as "the deepest trench
  on Earth".
- **The timeline**: 90 days of supplies; the station went silent 40 days
  before the dive and the beacon resumed 3 weeks after that. The logs are
  undated after Day 1.
